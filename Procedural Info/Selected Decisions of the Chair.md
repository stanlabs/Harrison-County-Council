# Council Parliamentary Journal
### Points of Order, Parliamentary Inquiries, Rulings, and Precedents

> **Purpose:** This document records points of order, parliamentary inquiries, rulings of the chair, and relevant parliamentary precedents arising during sessions of the County Council. It also contains commentary from the parliamentarian where clarification or further analysis is appropriate.
>
> **Scope:** Unless otherwise indicated, descriptions of rulings reflect the ruling as given by the presiding officer at the time of the session. A ruling recorded here does not necessarily represent the parliamentarian's opinion of the correct parliamentary rule.

# Third County Council

## September 13 Session

**Presiding Officer:** President _pro tempore_ `Vinnyvxx`

### Parliamentary Inquiry — Subsidiary Motions During Debate

A parliamentary inquiry was raised concerning whether amendments and other subsidiary motions may be made while a matter is under debate.

The **Chair's opinion** was that subsidiary motions are permissible during debate, including motions to:

- Amend;
- Refer or commit the matter to a committee; and
- Make other subsidiary motions permitted by the applicable rules.

**Ruling:** Subsidiary motions are permitted while the main motion is under debate.

Councilor **LordHenryVonHenry** raised a point of order concerning the determination of the vote.

The councilor argued that nominations could not be approved based upon a majority of the quorum, but instead required a majority calculated from the **total membership of the Council**.

### Point of Order — Transition from Executive to Legislative Business

Councilor **Vinnyvxx** raised a point of order after the Council, having tabled an executive appointment, attempted to consider matters relating to legislative business.

The councilor argued that the Council was required to formally enter legislative business before considering legislative matters.

County Executive `Bob_govv` **sustained the point of order** and ruled the motion out of order.

**Ruling:** A motion concerning legislative business was out of order while the Council remained in executive business.

> [!NOTE]
> **Parliamentarian's Observation:** RONR does **not** generally establish a distinction between "legislative business" and "executive business" as categories of ordinary parliamentary business. Those categories therefore should not be attributed to RONR without an independent rule establishing them. 
> The Council's own rules may establish an order of business or distinguish between different classes of matters.

### Point of Order — Proxy Votes on Verbal Two-Thirds Motions

Councilor **LordHenryVonHenry** raised a point of order concerning proxy voting on a motion requiring a two-thirds vote.

The councilor argued that allowing one councilor to cast a proxy vote on behalf of another councilor was impermissible where the motion had not been submitted in writing beforehand.

County Executive `Bob_govv` ruled that **proxy votes are prohibited on verbal motions that were not submitted in writing beforehand**.

**Ruling:** Proxy voting is prohibited for verbal motions that have not been submitted in writing in advance.

### Point of Order — Taking an Executive Nomination from the Table During Legislative Business

Councilor **LordHenryVonHenry** attempted to move to take an executive nomination from the table.

The Council was then conducting legislative business.

County Executive `Bob_govv` ruled the motion out of order on the grounds that it was not germane to the business then before the Council.

Councilor LordHenryVonHenry argued that a matter that has been laid on the table should be capable of being taken from the table regardless of which category of business the Council was currently conducting, and that it should not be necessary to return to executive business merely to take the matter from the table.

`Bob_govv` subsequently ruled that, under *Robert's Rules of Order*, the motion was permissible.

**Final Ruling:** The motion to take the nomination from the table was ruled **in order**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Parliamentarian's Note:** The chair did not identify a specific provision of *Robert's Rules of Order* supporting the ruling.
>
> Furthermore, RONR does **not** generally establish a distinction between "legislative business" and "executive business" as categories of ordinary parliamentary business. Those categories therefore should not be attributed to RONR without an independent rule establishing them.
>
> The Council's own rules may establish an order of business or distinguish between different classes of matters. If so, that authority comes from the Council's governing documents rather than from RONR itself.

> [!NOTE]
> **Executive Sessions — Historical and Parliamentary Context**
>
> The terminology of "executive business" or "executive session" has a distinct historical usage in legislative bodies, particularly the **United States Senate**, where executive sessions historically concerned presidential nominations and other matters requiring proceedings closed to the public.
>
> In a deliberative body, an executive session is not ordinarily presumed to exist merely because the body is considering an appointment or other non-legislative matter. It must derive from the body's governing law or rules of proceeding.
>
> Where an executive session is authorized, it generally refers to a portion of the body's proceedings from which the public is excluded for matters requiring confidentiality, such as:
>
> - Pending or potential litigation;
> - Personnel matters;
> - Disciplinary matters;
> - Confidential legal matters; or
> - Contract negotiations and other sensitive governmental business.
>
> Accordingly, **"executive business" is not a general category created by RONR** and should not be treated as such unless the Council's own rules establish it.

### Point of Order — Second Required for Unanimous Consent

Councilor **LordHenryVonHenry** raised a point of order arguing that motions made by unanimous consent require a second.

No ruling was issued by the chair.

**Parliamentarian's Opinion:** A motion presented by unanimous consent does **not** require a second under *Robert's Rules of Order Newly Revised*, 12th edition, § 4:59.

The same conclusion applies under the Council's Standing Rules, **Rules of Proceeding (Third County Council), § 6.3**.

**Precedent/Opinion:** A second is not required when the chair seeks unanimous consent.

> [!NOTE]
> A request for unanimous consent is procedurally distinct from an ordinary motion. The chair asks whether there is any objection to taking the proposed action. If no objection is heard, the action is taken without the ordinary requirements applicable to a formally made motion. The council should refrain from unanimous consent when doing the final adoption of ordinances. It is best practice to maintain a roll-call vote. The council may adopt ordinances in bulk by utilizing consent calendars.

# Second County Council

## Point of Order — Closing of the Gallery to the Public

**Presiding Officer:** County Executive `ChesapeakeZoom`

Following recent violent attacks, Presiding Officer `ChesapeakeZoom` ordered the gallery closed to the general public.

Councilor **Stanlabs** raised a point of order arguing that Council proceedings must remain open and visible to the public.

The presiding officer ruled that, because of the substantial threat to life and safety, members of the public could be removed from the gallery and the Council could continue conducting business.

**Ruling:** The public may be excluded from the gallery when necessary to address a substantial threat to life and safety, and the Council may continue conducting business thereafter.
