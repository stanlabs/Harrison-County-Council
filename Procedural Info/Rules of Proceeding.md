## PART ONE - GENERAL PROVISIONS


### 1.1 Preamble


These Rules of Proceeding are adopted under Article III of the [Harrison County Charter](https://hackmd.io/@HarrisonCountyRoblox/B1tQSrFSWl#CHARTER-OF-HARRISON-COUNTY-CHESAPEAKE) and govern the proceedings of the Harrison County Council. These Rules shall be followed together with the County Charter and applicable law. If these Rules conflict with the Charter or applicable law, the Charter or applicable law shall prevail. Any procedural question not addressed by the Charter, applicable law, or these Rules shall be decided by the Chair, subject to appeal to the Council.

### 1.2 Definitions


For purposes of these Rules, the following terms shall have the following meanings:

* **Council**: the Harrison County Council.
* **Councilor**: a voting member of the Council.
* **County Executive**: the person holding the office of County Executive pursuant to the County Charter, including when acting as President or Chair of the Council as provided by the Charter.
* **Chair**: the person lawfully presiding over a session.
* **County Clerk**: the person holding office pursuant to 7 H.C.S. § 2102, including any person lawfully acting in that capacity.
* **County Clerk's Office**: the Office of the County Clerk established pursuant to 7 H.C.S. § 2101, including the Clerk and subordinate officers and staff serving within the Office.
* Council Officers and Staff: any officer, employee, or staff member appointed by the Council pursuant to Section 20 of the Charter, excluding Councilors, the County Executive, the Clerk, and Clerk’s Staff unless otherwise expressly provided.
* **Council Staff**: a collective term for persons serving in, assisting, or officially participating in the administration or proceedings of the Council, including the County Executive, Councilors, the County Clerk, staff of the County Clerk’s Office, Council Officers and Staff, and any other person lawfully designated to perform an official Council function. Use of the term Council Staff does not imply that every person included within the term is an employee of, subordinate to, or appointed by the Council.
* **Security Personnel**: Deputies of the Harrison County Sheriff’s Office assigned to provide security to the Council; other peace officers lawfully assisting with security at a Council session, hearing, committee meeting, or other official proceeding; and any security officer or employee lawfully employed or assigned by the Council to provide security. The term does not grant any authority beyond that otherwise provided by law.
* **Bill**: legislation intended to become law pursuant to the Charter. Although the Charter refers to such legislation as a bill, bills are commonly referred to by the Council, County government, and the public as **ordinances**. Unless the context requires otherwise, the terms bill and ordinance may be used interchangeably in Council business, official records, and public communications.
* **Resolution**: formal legislative action having force only within the legislative branch.
* **Session**: a duly convened meeting of the Council.
* **Simple Majority**: a majority of the Councilors present and voting at a session at which a quorum is present.
* Whole Council: all Councilors then holding office, excluding any vacant seat, unless the Charter or applicable law expressly requires calculation based upon the full authorized membership of seven Councilors.

### 1.3 Order of Authority


Questions of Council procedure shall be resolved according to the following order:

1. the County Charter and other superior law;
2. applicable County law;
3. these Rules;
4. established Council precedent consistent with the above; and
5. generally recognized principles of parliamentary procedure.

## PART TWO - OFFICERS, STAFF, AND ADMINISTRATION


### 2.1 Presiding Officer


The County Executive shall serve as the normal President and presiding officer of the Council. While presiding, the County Executive shall oversee the procedural conduct of the session and shall remain a non-voting member of the Council except when casting a tiebreaking vote as provided by the Charter.

If the County Executive is not present when the Council convenes, or is otherwise unable to preside, the President *pro tempore* shall preside. If neither the County Executive nor the President *pro tempore* is present and able to preside, the Councilors present shall elect one Councilor to serve as Chair; provided, however, that such Councilor shall relinquish the chair upon the County Executive or President *pro tempore* becoming present and able to preside.

The person presiding over a session pursuant to this Rule shall be referred to as the Chair.

### 2.2 Duties of the Chair


The Chair shall:

* call the Council to order;
* determine whether a quorum is present;
* announce the business before the Council;
* recognize persons entitled to speak;
* preserve order and decorum;
* rule on points of order and procedural questions;
* put motions and questions to a vote;
* announce the result of votes;
* enforce these Rules; and
* perform other procedural duties required by the Charter, applicable law, or these Rules.

The Chair shall apply these Rules impartially.

### 2.3 President *pro tempore*


As the first order of business of each Council term, the Council shall, by resolution, elect one Councilor to serve as President *pro tempore*.

The President *pro tempore* shall serve as the vice president of the Council and shall serve as Chair and preside over Council sessions if the County Executive is not present when the Council convenes or is otherwise unable to preside. The President *pro tempore* shall also perform such other duties as may be assigned by the County Executive or these Rules.

A President *pro tempore* who is presiding shall retain all rights of a councilor, including but not limited to the right to vote, but shall not use the authority of the Chair to give himself or herself an unfair procedural advantage.

A serving President *pro tempore* may be removed upon the affirmative vote of three-fourths of the Council, after which the Council may elect a new President *pro tempore* by resolution.

### 2.4 County Clerk and County Clerk's Office


The County Clerk and County Clerk's Office shall exercise the duties and authority established by [law](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1djyPYIhVZFxuS9ifpqu85d2Zr0T18ndRk9tNrE__BAY/edit?gid=461250397#gid=461250397&range=A48:I48).

The County Clerk may advise the Chair, Councilors, and other Council Staff concerning records, procedure, docket status, and prior Council practice.

The absence of the County Clerk shall not prevent the Council from meeting. A member of the County Clerk’s Office or another person lawfully designated may perform necessary clerical duties for that session.

### 2.5 Other Officers and Staff


Pursuant to Section 20 of the Charter, the Council may appoint such other officers and staff as it determines necessary for the conduct of Council business.

The Council may establish their positions, duties, responsibilities, organization, and supervision by resolution, rule, policy, or other lawful action. All such officers and staff shall serve and perform their duties in accordance with the Charter, applicable law, these Rules, and lawful directives of the Council.

### 2.6 General Expectations of Council Staff


Council Staff shall perform official Council functions in an orderly, professional, and impartial manner appropriate to their respective offices or roles.

Council Staff shall cooperate with the County Clerk's Office as necessary to enable the County Clerk's Office to perform its duties pursuant to law, these Rules, and lawful directives of the Council.

### 2.7 Council Security


The Harrison County Sheriff’s Office shall have primary responsibility for providing security for Council sessions, hearings, committee meetings, and other official Council proceedings. Additional Security Personnel may provide or assist with security as authorized by law.

Security Personnel may:

* maintain the safety and security of Council chambers and other official meeting areas;
* preserve order when requested by the Chair;
* control access to restricted or secured areas;
* enforce lawful orders concerning removal from a proceeding;
* protect Council Staff, members of the public, witnesses, and other persons present; and
* exercise any other authority otherwise granted by law.

The Chair may request reasonable assistance from Security Personnel during a proceeding but shall not direct Security Personnel to take an action that is unlawful or inconsistent with the Charter.

Nothing in these Rules places peace officers under the law-enforcement command of the Chair or Council, except that they may carry out lawful security-related directions concerning the conduct and security of the proceeding.

## PART THREE - ORGANIZATION AND SESSIONS


### 3.1 First Session of a Term


At the first session of each Council term, business shall proceed as follows:

1. call to order;
2. roll call;
3. determination of quorum;
4. election of the President *pro tempore* as the first order of business;
5. adoption of the Rules of Proceeding by resolution as the second order of business;
6. other organizational business; and
7. regular Council business, if any.

No other substantive business shall precede the election of the President *pro tempore* or adoption of the Rules.

### 3.2 Regular Sessions


Pursuant to the Charter, the Council shall sit in session at least once every seven days.

The Council may establish a regular session schedule, and may authorize one break during each Council term during which no session is required, provided that such break does not exceed fourteen days.

### 3.3 Special Sessions


A special session may be called by any Councilor for reasonable cause.

The call shall state the purpose of the special session and identify the business to be considered.

A special session shall generally be limited to the business stated in the call, except that additional business may be considered where permitted by law and agreed to by the Council.

### 3.4 Quorum


For purposes of Section 21 of the Charter, the voting members of the Council shall consist of all Councilors then holding office, excluding vacant seats. A majority of the voting members shall constitute a quorum to do business.

No substantive Council action may be taken without a quorum. In the absence of a quorum, those present may:

* recess;
* take reasonable measures to secure attendance;
* arrange another session; or
* adjourn.

Any Councilor may raise the absence of a quorum.

### 3.5 Attendance


The County Clerk's Office shall record attendance at the beginning of each session.

A Councilor arriving after the session begins shall be recorded as present from the time of arrival.

A Councilor leaving before adjournment should notify the Chair or County Clerk's Office.

A Councilor who is disconnected, away, or otherwise unavailable when a vote occurs shall not be recorded as voting.

The Council may excuse a Councilor from attendance, and the attendance records shall identify excused absences where applicable.

### 3.6 Failure to Attend


A Councilor who fails to attend sittings of the Council for three consecutive weeks without the consent of the Council is automatically expelled as provided by the Charter.

Nothing in these Rules may waive or alter that Charter requirement.

Where the members of the Council have failed to meet within the time required by the Charter, or are purposefully absent from a session, the County Executive or President *pro tempore* may petition the Courts of Justice for a warrant as provided by the Charter.

## PART FOUR - AGENDA AND ORDER OF BUSINESS


### 4.1 Council Agenda


As provided by the Charter, the Council controls its own agenda, and the County Executive shall not set or control the agenda, including while presiding.

The County Clerk's Office may prepare and publish the agenda administratively based upon:

* unfinished business;
* properly submitted bills, ordinances, and resolutions;
* nominations and appointments;
* matters ordered by the Council; and
* other business properly submitted for Council consideration.

Any Councilor may submit business for placement on the agenda.

The Council may change the order of the agenda by majority vote.

The Chair may make minor procedural adjustments to the order of business where necessary for orderly proceedings, provided that properly pending business is not improperly withheld.

### 4.2 Order of Business


Unless the Council orders otherwise, regular sessions shall proceed substantially as follows:

1. call to order;
2. roll call and determination of quorum;
3. approval or correction of the Journal, where necessary;
4. unfinished business;
5. reports and communications;
6. appointments and nominations;
7. bills, ordinances, and resolutions;
8. other new business;
9. public audience;
10. announcements, if any; and
11. adjournment.

### 4.3 Order of Consideration


Matters shall ordinarily be considered in docket order.

### 4.4 Unfinished Business


Business left pending at adjournment may be considered as unfinished business at the next session.

Tabled business shall not automatically return to the floor and must first be taken from the table.

## PART FIVE - RIGHTS OF MEMBERS AND DEBATE


### 5.1 Legislative Privilege


The rights and protections of Councilors under Section 17 of the Charter and other law shall not be limited by these Rules.

No Councilor shall be questioned, penalized, arrested, or otherwise held to account in another place for speech, debate, votes, or statements made in the course of an official legislative proceeding, as provided by the Charter.

### 5.2 Recognition


A Councilor wishing to speak shall seek recognition from the Chair.

A Councilor shall not proceed until recognized except where these Rules permit interruption.

If multiple Councilors seek recognition, the Chair shall determine who is entitled to the floor.

Recognition shall be administered fairly.

### 5.3 Debate


Debate shall relate to the question before the Council.

Members shall address remarks through the Chair.

Members shall avoid:

* personal attacks;
* disorderly or abusive conduct;
* deliberate disruption;
* excessive repetition; and
* unrelated discussion.

Unless otherwise ordered, a Councilor shall not speak for more than five consecutive minutes during one recognition.

A Councilor should not receive a second substantive opportunity to speak until other Councilors seeking recognition have been given a reasonable opportunity.

### 5.4 Yielding


A Councilor holding the floor may voluntarily yield:

* for a question;
* for information;
* briefly to another member; or
* the remainder of the floor.

A Councilor shall not be required to yield except when debate has been properly ended or the Councilor has been called to order.

### 5.5 Point of Order


A Councilor may raise a point of order when they believe the Charter, applicable law, or these Rules are being violated.

The Councilor shall briefly state the alleged violation.

The Chair shall rule on the point.

A point of order may interrupt another speaker when immediate consideration is reasonably necessary.

### 5.6 Parliamentary Inquiry


A Councilor may ask the Chair a parliamentary inquiry concerning:

* proper procedure;
* the effect of a motion;
* the vote required;
* the status of pending business; or
* another procedural matter.

A parliamentary inquiry does not itself challenge a ruling of the Chair.

### 5.7 Appeal of the Chair


A Councilor may appeal a procedural ruling of the Chair, with a second.

The Chair and Councilor making the appeal may briefly explain their positions before debate and voting.

The Council may permit limited debate.

The question shall be: “Shall the decision of the Chair be sustained?”

A majority vote against sustaining the Chair shall overturn the ruling. If the vote is tied, the tie shall be resolved in accordance with Section 19 of the Charter.

### 5.8 Decorum and Discipline


Council Staff shall conduct themselves in an orderly and professional manner during official proceedings.

The Chair may call a Councilor or other member of Council Staff to order for violating these Rules.

The Chair should ordinarily warn the person before taking further procedural action, however a Councilor who continues disorderly conduct may:

* lose the floor;
* be directed to take their seat; or
* be subject to further action by the Council.

Other Council Staff who engage in disorderly conduct may be directed to cease the conduct or, where lawful and appropriate, leave the proceeding.

A person who refuses a lawful direction to leave, or whose conduct requires removal for the preservation of order or safety, may be removed by Security Personnel at the direction of the Chair or as otherwise authorized by law.

Security Personnel shall not remove, detain, or otherwise interfere with a Councilor solely because of the Councilor’s speech, debate, vote, statement, or lawful participation in an official legislative proceeding.

Nothing in this Rule limits the lawful authority of Security Personnel to respond to criminal conduct, threats, violence, emergencies, or other circumstances requiring immediate action.

The Chair may not unilaterally suspend, expel, or deprive a Councilor of their right to vote.

Formal discipline or expulsion of a Councilor may occur only as authorized by the Charter or applicable law.

## PART SIX - MOTIONS


### 6.1 Generally


A Councilor may make a motion after being recognized. Unless otherwise provided, a motion requires a second. A second means only that another Councilor wishes the motion to be considered. After a motion is seconded, the Chair shall state the motion before the Council.

A motion may be ruled out of order if it:

* violates the Charter or applicable law;
* is outside the authority of the Council;
* violates these Rules;
* is clearly dilatory or intended solely to obstruct proceedings; or
* attempts to repeatedly decide a question already finally resolved.

A ruling that a motion is out of order may be appealed.

A motion not specifically listed in this Part may nevertheless be considered at the reasonable discretion of the Chair where the purpose and requested action of the motion are sufficiently clear and the motion is otherwise consistent with the Charter, applicable law, and these Rules.

Unless otherwise required by the Charter, applicable law, or these Rules, an unlisted motion shall:

* require a second;
* be debatable; and
* require a simple majority vote for adoption.

### 6.3 Unanimous Consent


Business, typically routine or uncontroversial, may be handled by unanimous consent.

The Chair shall ask whether there is an objection.

If no Councilor objects, the proposed action is adopted. If any Councilor objects, the matter shall proceed under the ordinary rules.

### 6.4 Recess


A Councilor may move to recess for a stated period.

The motion requires a second and majority vote.

The Chair may order a brief recess without motion where immediately necessary because of:

* a technical problem;
* disorder;
* a safety issue; or
* another temporary circumstance preventing orderly proceedings.

The Council shall automatically enter emergency recess, without motion, vote, or action of the Chair, upon the occurrence of an emergency materially affecting the safety or security of the proceeding, including:

* an active threat;
* a shooting;
* a serious injury;
* a death;
* a fire;
* an evacuation;
* a medical emergency; or
* another similar circumstance.

During an emergency recess, all Council business and debate shall immediately cease, and no vote or other substantive action shall be taken.

The emergency recess shall continue until the emergency has ended and the Chair calls the Council back to order, or until the session is otherwise lawfully adjourned.

### 6.5 Withdrawal or Modification of a Motion


The maker of a motion may withdraw or modify the motion at any time before voting on the motion has begun.

Once voting has begun, the motion may not be withdrawn or modified except through another procedure authorized by these Rules.

### 6.6 Reconsideration of a Motion


A Councilor who voted on the prevailing side may move to reconsider a vote.

The motion must be made:

* during the same session; or
* at the next regular session.

The motion requires a second and simple majority vote.

A question may not repeatedly be reconsidered after reconsideration has been finally disposed of.

### 6.7 Rescind Previous Action


The Council may rescind or amend a previous action where permitted by law.

The motion requires a second and is debatable.

Adoption requires:

* a two-thirds vote; or
* a majority of the Whole Council if advance notice was given.

A law enacted through a bill or ordinance shall be amended or repealed through another bill or ordinance where required by law.

### 6.8 Suspension of the Rules


A motion to suspend the Rules shall identify:

* the specific provision or provisions proposed to be suspended; and
* a brief justification for the suspension.

The Council may not suspend:

* the County Charter or applicable law;
* any voting threshold imposed by the Charter or applicable law;
* any rule governing quorum;
* any rule governing the voting rights of Councilors or the County Executive;
* any rule governing legislative privilege;
* any Charter-guaranteed right of audience;
* any rule governing automatic emergency recess;
* any rule prohibiting the County Executive from setting or controlling the Council agenda;
* any Charter-derived rule governing the President *pro tempore*;
* Part Seven, Rules 7.1 through 7.3, governing the forms, meaning, and legal effect of bills, ordinances, and resolutions;
* any requirement of Part Seven necessary to preserve the official filing, text, or legislative record of a measure;
* any rule governing the presentation, signing, return, veto, or automatic enactment of a bill or ordinance;
* any Charter-derived rule governing expulsion;
* Part Sixteen in its entirety;
* this Rule; or
* any other provision expressly designated by these Rules as non-suspendable.

## PART SEVEN - BILLS, ORDINANCES, AND RESOLUTIONS


### 7.1 Forms of Legislation


Pursuant to the County Charter, Council legislation shall formally be set forth as either:

* a Bill; or
* a Resolution.

For purposes of ordinary Council business, County government usage, public communication, docketing, citation, and legislative records, a bill may be referred to as an ordinance. The use of the term ordinance does not create a separate form of legislation from a bill. An ordinance is a bill within the meaning of the Charter and is subject to all Charter requirements applicable to bills.

The Rules of Proceeding shall be adopted and amended by resolution.

### 7.2 Bills and Ordinances


A bill, commonly referred to as an ordinance, is legislation intended to become County law under the Charter.

Bills or ordinances may create, amend, repeal, or otherwise exercise legislative authority.

A bill or ordinance passed by the Council shall be presented to the County Executive as required by the Charter.

References in these Rules to a bill, ordinance, or bill or ordinance refer to the same form of legislation unless the context clearly provides otherwise.

The County Clerk's Office may use the term ordinance in legislative numbering, docket entries, records, publications, and other official administrative uses consistent with established County practice.

### 7.3 Resolutions


A resolution is formal Council action having force only within the legislative branch.

Resolutions may be used for:

* internal Council matters;
* Council rules;
* appointments or actions within Council authority;
* statements or declarations of the Council;
* exercises of authority the Charter or County law expressly directs to be accomplished by resolution; and
* other legislative-branch matters not requiring enactment as County law.

A resolution is not presented to the County Executive for signature unless otherwise required by the Charter or applicable law.

### 7.4 Introduction


Any Councilor may introduce a bill, ordinance, or resolution.

A proposed measure shall be submitted to the County Clerk's Office [in a manner prescribed by the County Clerk](https://discord.com/channels/1460643972606066730/1520766619876462833).

The County Clerk's Office should place the submitted legislation on the docket within twenty-four (24) to forty-eight (48) hours of submission.

The County Clerk's Office may establish reasonable filing, formatting, and docketing requirements consistent with law.

### 7.5 Consideration


When a bill, ordinance, or resolution comes before the Council:

* its number and title shall be announced;
* the sponsor may explain the measure;
* Councilors may ask questions;
* amendments may be offered;
* debate shall be permitted; and
* the Council shall vote on final passage or adoption.

The entire text need not be read aloud unless required by law or ordered by the Council.

### 7.6 Amendments


A substantive amendment to a bill, ordinance, or resolution should be submitted in writing.

The County Clerk's Office shall maintain the official amended text.

Amendments must relate to the general subject of the measure.

The final vote shall be upon the measure as amended.

The County Clerk's Office may make non-substantive corrections to spelling, grammar, numbering, formatting, citations, cross-references, typographical errors, and similar clerical matters where the correction does not materially alter the substance, legal effect, or intent of the measure.

### 7.7 Withdrawal of Legislation


A sponsor may withdraw a bill, ordinance, or resolution before substantive consideration begins.

After substantive consideration begins, withdrawal requires consent of the Council.

If a measure has multiple sponsors, one sponsor may remove their name without withdrawing the measure if another sponsor wishes to proceed.

### 7.8 Final Passage


A bill, ordinance, or resolution passes upon receiving the vote required by the Charter or applicable law.

Unless another requirement applies, passage requires a majority of votes cast while a quorum is present.

The County Clerk's Office shall record the vote and final disposition.

## PART EIGHT - VOTING


### 8.1 Voting Rights


Each Councilor has one vote.

A Councilor serving as Chair retains their vote.

The County Executive shall not vote except to break a tie while personally presiding over the session.

### 8.2 Ties


If a vote is tied while the County Executive is presiding, the County Executive shall cast the deciding vote.

If a Councilor is presiding, a tied measure or motion fails.

The County Executive has no tie-breaking vote while not presiding.

### 8.3 Methods of Voting


Votes may ordinarily be taken by voice vote.

A roll-call vote shall be taken:

* when required by law;
* when ordered by the Chair; or
* upon request of any Councilor.

During a roll-call vote, the vote of each Councilor shall be individually recorded.

### 8.4 Recording Votes


Councilors may be recorded as:

* **Aye**: the Councilor voted in favor of the question.
* **Nay**: the Councilor voted against the question.
* **Abstain**: the Councilor was present and eligible to vote but expressly chose not to cast an affirmative or negative vote.
* **No Vote**: the Councilor was present or otherwise participating when the vote was taken but did not cast a vote and did not expressly abstain.
* **Recused**: the Councilor did not participate in the vote because they were disqualified, required to refrain from participation, or otherwise properly recused from the matter.
* **Absent**: the Councilor was not present, available, or participating when the vote was taken.

The County Clerk's Office shall record the result of each vote and, where a roll-call vote is taken, the individual vote or status of each Councilor.

### 8.5 Abstention and Recusal


A Councilor may abstain where permitted by law.

Permission of the Chair is not required to abstain or recuse.

A Councilor shall recuse where required by law.

The County Clerk’s Office shall separately record recusals.

### 8.6 Absentee and Proxy Voting


A Councilor who knows that they will be unable to attend a session, or who during a session knows that they will be unable to personally cast a vote on one or more matters, may designate another Councilor to serve as their proxy.

A proxy designation shall identify the Councilor authorized to act on behalf of the designating Councilor and may apply:

* to an entire session;
* to one or more specified matters;
* for a stated period of time; or
* until revoked by the Councilor.

Only a Councilor then holding office may serve as a proxy.

A Councilor lawfully designated as a proxy may cast votes on behalf of the designating Councilor within the scope of the designation.

The designating Councilor may provide specific voting instructions to the proxy or may authorize the proxy to exercise discretion on the designating Councilor’s behalf.

A proxy designation should be communicated to the Chair or County Clerk’s Office before the proxy vote is cast and shall be entered into the Council record.

A Councilor may revoke or modify a proxy designation at any time before the proxy has cast the affected vote.

A proxy vote shall be recorded as the vote of the Councilor who designated the proxy, with the record noting which Councilor cast the vote by proxy.

A proxy does not create an additional voting member and shall not independently count toward quorum. For purposes of quorum, the underlying Councilor shall be counted only if the Charter, applicable law, or these Rules otherwise permit that Councilor to be treated as present or participating.

## PART NINE - EXECUTIVE ACTION ON BILLS AND ORDINANCES


### 9.1 Presentation


After a bill or ordinance passes the Council, the County Clerk's Office shall present it to the County Executive [in a manner prescribed by the County Clerk](https://discord.com/channels/1460643972606066730/1541870315930521631).

The County Clerk's Office shall record the date of presentation.

The County Executive may:

* sign the bill or ordinance, in which case it becomes law; or
* return it to the Council with objections.

### 9.2 Executive Time Limit


A bill or ordinance not signed or returned by the end of the third day following presentation automatically becomes law without the County Executive’s signature.

The County Clerk's Office shall record when a bill or ordinance becomes law under this Rule.

### 9.3 Returned Bills


When the County Executive returns a bill or ordinance with objections, the County Clerk's Office shall enter the objections into the Council record and make them available to the Councilors.

The Council may reconsider the returned measure.

A returned bill or ordinance becomes law notwithstanding the County Executive's objections if approved by all Councilors then holding office, less one, as provided by the Charter.

The County Clerk's Office shall certify and record a successful override.

## PART TEN - NOMINATIONS AND APPOINTMENTS


### 10.1 Nominations


A nomination requiring Council action shall be submitted to the County Clerk’s Office and entered upon the docket promptly.

When considered:

* the nomination shall be announced;
* the nominee may give an opening statement to the Council;
* Councilors may ask relevant questions;
* debate may occur; and
* the Council shall vote.

The vote required shall be determined by the Charter or applicable law. If no vote requirement is otherwise established, approval shall require a Simple Majority.

A nomination may be withdrawn at any time before voting has begun.

### 10.2 Appointments


*Reserved for future use.*

## PART ELEVEN - COMMITTEES AND OVERSIGHT


### 11.1 Committees


The Council may create committees and appoint their members pursuant to the Charter.

A committee may be standing, special, select, investigative, or another type authorized by the Council.

The measure creating a committee should identify its:

* purpose;
* membership;
* leadership;
* authority; and
* duration, if temporary.

A majority of a committee’s members constitutes a quorum unless the Council provides otherwise.

### 11.2 Committee Procedure


Committees shall follow these Rules where reasonably applicable.

Committees may:

* consider matters referred to them;
* conduct hearings;
* gather information;
* issue reports;
* make recommendations; and
* exercise other authority granted by the Charter or Council.

The County Clerk’s Office may provide clerical, recordkeeping, and procedural support to committees.

Committee action does not bind the full Council unless the Council formally adopts or ratifies it where required.

### 11.3 Inquiries and Investigations


The Council may open inquiries or investigations into the conduct or operations of any department, branch, or officer of the County.

The Council may assign an inquiry or investigation to a committee.

The Council or an authorized committee may request testimony, documents, records, or other information relevant to the inquiry.

Council Staff may assist with the organization, documentation, and administration of an inquiry where authorized.

Compulsory process may be used only to the extent authorized by law.

11. 4 Required Attendance of Certain Officials

The Council may require any employee or officer of the County to attend a meeting of the Council or one of its committees.

Council action requiring attendance should identify:

* the person or office required to appear;
* the meeting or hearing;
* the date and time, where known; and
* the general subject for which attendance is required.

The County Clerk’s Office shall provide appropriate notice.

11. 5 Other Charter Powers

The Council may exercise the additional powers granted by the Charter, including:

* ending a declared state of emergency and the use of emergency powers by declared resolution;
* ratifying treaties and other formal agreements relating to foreign affairs; and
* exercising any other power granted to the Council by the Charter or applicable law.

## PART TWELVE - RIGHTS OF AUDIENCE AND PUBLIC ACCESS


### 12.1 Right of Audience - Residents


A resident of Harrison County may apply to be heard by the Council in writing or by speech at a Council meeting.

A resident who properly applies shall be allowed not less than two minutes to address the Council. The Council may allow additional time.

The Chair and/or County Clerk’s Office may establish reasonable administrative procedures concerning:

* applications;
* order of speakers;
* recognition;
* submission of written statements; and
* maintenance of orderly records.

Such procedures shall not be used to defeat the right of audience guaranteed by the Charter.

### 12.2 Right of Audience - Charter Officials


A government official whose position is established in the Charter shall have a similar right of audience when speaking on behalf of their official position or agency.

Such an official shall be allowed not less than four minutes. The Council may allow additional time.

### 12.3 Conduct During Public Speaking


Speakers shall address the Council in an orderly manner.

The Chair may enforce reasonable time limits and rules of decorum.

Criticism of the Council, Council Staff, County officials, legislation, policies, or governmental action shall not by itself constitute disorderly conduct.

A speaker may be stopped for conduct that substantially disrupts the session or prevents the Council from proceeding.

### 12.4 Public Access


Council sessions shall be open for public observation except where closure is authorized by law.

Spectators shall not substantially disrupt proceedings.

The Chair may order a disruptive spectator removed after warning where reasonably practicable.

Reasonable accommodations may be made for members of the press.

Council Staff may take reasonable administrative measures necessary to maintain orderly access to the meeting and official Council areas.

## PART THIRTEEN - RECORDS


### 13.1 Council Journal


The County Clerk’s Office shall maintain an official Journal containing, as applicable:

* the date and time the session convened and adjourned;
* Councilors present and absent;
* motions;
* bills, ordinances, and resolutions considered;
* amendments;
* nominations and appointments;
* material rulings of the Chair;
* votes;
* recesses;
* actions of the Council; and
* other information required by law.

The Journal need not contain a word-for-word transcript of debate.

### 13.2 Correction of the Council Journal


A Councilor may request correction of an error in the Journal.

The County Clerk’s Office may correct clerical errors.

A disputed substantive correction shall be decided by the Council.

### 13.3 Official Legislative Records


The County Clerk’s Office shall maintain the official copy and status of each bill, ordinance, resolution, amendment, nomination, and other official Council filing.

Council Staff shall provide the County Clerk’s Office with official records, votes, amendments, filings, or other materials within their possession that are reasonably necessary for complete Council records.

Where conflicting copies exist, the official version maintained or certified by the County Clerk’s Office shall control, subject to correction of demonstrated error.

## PART FOURTEEN - EXPULSION AND MEMBER DISCIPLINE


### 14.1 Automatic Expulsion for Absence


A Councilor who fails to attend sittings of the Council for three consecutive weeks without the consent of the Council shall be automatically expelled pursuant to the Charter.

The County Clerk’s Office shall maintain attendance records sufficient to determine whether this requirement has been triggered.

### 14.2 Expulsion for Misconduct


The Council may expel a Councilor for willful misconduct or other good cause.

Expulsion requires the approval of all Councilors then holding office, less one, as provided by the Charter.

Before the vote, the Councilor concerned shall:

* receive reasonable notice of the grounds;
* be allowed to respond; and
* be permitted to participate to the extent required by law.

A person expelled from the Council is ineligible to hold the office of Councilor for one hundred days following expulsion.

## PART FIFTEEN - END OF TERM


### 15.1 Unfinished Business


Unless otherwise provided by law, unfinished business expires at the conclusion of the Council term.

This includes:

* bills or ordinances not finally passed;
* resolutions not finally adopted;
* pending nominations;
* tabled measures;
* pending motions; and
* other unfinished questions.

The succeeding Council may reintroduce an expired matter.

Council Staff shall ensure that official records and continuing administrative matters are preserved and transferred appropriately between Council terms.

## PART SIXTEEN - AMENDMENT AND INTERPRETATION


### 16.1 Interpretation


The Chair shall initially rule upon procedural questions concerning these Rules during a session. A procedural ruling of the Chair may be appealed to the Council.

The County Clerk or Clerk’s Office may provide information concerning the text of these Rules, Council records, prior procedure, and the procedural status of business.

Neither the Chair, County Clerk, nor Council may interpret these Rules in a manner inconsistent with the Charter or applicable law.

### 16.2 Amendment to the Rules


These Rules may be amended at any time by resolution of the Council.

At least twenty-four hours’ notice shall ordinarily be provided before consideration.

Adoption requires a majority vote unless a greater vote is required by the Charter, applicable law, or these Rules.

An amendment takes effect upon adoption unless the resolution provides otherwise.

### 16.3 Suspension of Administrative Requirements


Administrative requirements established by the County Clerk’s Office may be waived or modified at the discretion of the County Clerk, as necessary for the orderly or efficient administration of Council business.

### 16.4 Severability


If any provision of these Rules is invalid or unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall remain effective to the greatest extent permitted by law.

### 16.5 Effective Date


These Rules shall take effect upon adoption by resolution of the Harrison County Council and shall supersede all prior Rules of Proceeding.
